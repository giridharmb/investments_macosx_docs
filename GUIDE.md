Open any of them from *Help & Money Map* or *Profiles*, look around and compare with your own plan, then switch back from the top of the sidebar. **Reset sample** undoes changes to the one you're in, and **Start my own from scratch** makes a blank profile of your own. Anyone from 13 up can set up a profile; under 18, some accounts need a parent to open them.

New to money words? Start with **Help & Money Map** — it shows how everything fits together.

Only four things are **required** for meaningful results:

1. Country, age and retirement age
2. Your paycheck / income
3. Rent or housing cost
4. A few monthly expenses

Everything else is **recommended** (debts, savings, investments, state) or **optional** (W-2, filing status, Social Security estimate, goals, other assets). The **Inputs Checklist** screen shows what's missing and what each input unlocks.

### Menu bar extra
A chart icon in the macOS menu bar opens a compact panel for the active profile:
net worth, take-home, spending, savings rate, free cash flow and emergency-fund months; the top warning; a **profile switcher**; a **hide/show amounts** toggle; **quick add expense** (amount, category, note — press Return); and buttons to open the app, Settings (⌘,) or quit.
Turn it off in *Settings → Appearance → Show in menu bar*.

## Widgets, Siri & Shortcuts

Add the **Money at a glance** widget from the desktop's *Edit Widgets* (or Notification Center): small shows net worth and spent this month; medium adds free cash flow, budget left and an *Add expense* button. Tapping opens the app on the Dashboard or Budget. With *Hide amounts* or App Lock on, widgets show •••• instead of numbers.

Say **"Log an expense in WealthPilot"** to add a purchase by voice (amount, then optionally what it was and a category), or **"What's my net worth in WealthPilot"** for net worth, this month's spending and budget left. Both actions are also in the Shortcuts app.

## Profiles

A **profile** is a complete, independent plan: its own country and assumptions, income, expenses, debts, savings, investments, goals, W-2s, net-worth history — and therefore its own results. Use them for:

- separate people or households (you, parents, a client),
- what-if scenarios (“Move to Texas”, “Retire at 50”, “Buy a house in 2028”),
- a sandbox with sample data.

**Default** is created automatically. It can be renamed and restyled but not deleted.

- **Switch** from the profile button at the top of the sidebar, or from the Profiles screen. Every screen immediately shows the selected profile.
- **Create** (Profiles → New profile): start *blank*, as a *copy of the current profile* (ideal for what-ifs), or with *sample data*. Pick a name, icon and color.
- **Manage** from each profile card's ⋯ menu: rename & style, duplicate, export, delete (removes the profile and all its data).
- **Compare**: with two or more profiles, the Profiles screen charts net worth, projected retirement balance and target, and lists key results side by side.
- **Backups**: export one profile or all profiles. When importing, add the backup as a new profile or replace the current one.
- **iCloud**: all profiles sync. Each device remembers which profile it has open.

## Reading the charts
- **Long charts** (net worth, projections) show a window of history. **Drag** with the mouse, **swipe sideways** on the trackpad or hold **⇧ while scrolling** to move through time, or use the **‹ › Latest** buttons under the chart.
- **Click** a bar or point to see its details; click it again (or move the chart) to dismiss.
- Shorter charts: drag across them to scrub through values.

## Moving around
- **Moving between screens:** buttons inside a screen that take you elsewhere (e.g. *Ask the AI advisor* on the Dashboard, *Add* in the Inputs checklist) open the new screen on top — **Back** returns to where you were.
- **Your settings stay put:** sliders, pickers and what-ifs on every screen are saved with the profile as you change them — no Save button needed — so leaving a screen (or the app) and coming back shows them as you left them, and with iCloud sync they're on your other devices too. They're also in backups and copied with a profile. The note under the sliders says *Saving…*, then *Saved · in iCloud* (or *Saved on this device*).
- **Scenarios:** on Projections, Rent vs Buy, each investment strategy and every calculator, open **Scenarios → Save as scenario…** to keep the current settings under a name, such as *Retire at 55* or *$600K house*. Pick a saved scenario from the same menu to switch back to it (the button shows which one you're on), or delete it. Saving with an existing name replaces that scenario.

## Filling in forms
- **Required vs optional:** fields marked **\*** are required; everything else is optional. If you don't know something yet, leave it blank (or 0) — the app uses a sensible default and you can fill it in later.
- **Hints:** the grey text under a field says what to enter and where to find it.
- **Checks:** at the bottom of a form, *Fix before saving* lists problems that keep Save switched off (e.g. no amount, an end date before the start, deductions bigger than the paycheck, a price alert *below* that's higher than *above*); *Worth a second look* lists likely typos you can still save (extra zeros, a payment that never pays the loan off, a balance over the card limit, a yearly rate that looks monthly).
- **What does this mean?** Click **ⓘ** next to a field for a plain-English explanation, an example and where to find the number. Every screen also has **?** in its toolbar: what the screen is for, how to fill it in, the words it uses and where it sits on the money map.
- **Don't know?** Every amount has a **Don't know** button. The field then shows *Don't know* and the app uses 0 — or a typical value where there is one (Social Security and Medicare tax on a W-2, salary growth 3%, a 4% withdrawal rate, the regional inflation rate). Even a required amount can be *Don't know*: the form saves with a reminder instead of an error. The **Inputs Checklist** lists every *Don't know* answer so you can come back to it; click **I know it now** to type the real number.
- **Cancel** undoes everything you changed in the form, including your own fields and *Don't know* answers.
- **Your own fields:** *Add your own field…* on a form adds a field to every form of that kind in the profile (see *Categories → Your form fields*).

## Accessibility
WealthPilot works with VoiceOver: buttons and charts have spoken labels, decorative icons are skipped, and section titles are headings you can jump between. Text follows your text-size setting (the money map's circles keep a fixed size so they don't overlap — the details below them grow). Warning text and colored buttons use darker shades so they're readable for low vision, in light and dark mode. Every screen is checked with Apple's accessibility audit.

## Choosing what you see
Not everyone needs every screen — a student may never use W-2s or retirement tax planning. Choose a layout when you set up a profile, or tap **Customize what you see…** at the bottom of the sidebar (also in Settings → Appearance):
- **Start from** a preset: Simple, Student, Working professional, Freelancer / self-employed, Family & home, Near or in retirement, or Everything.
- **Switch screens** on or off individually (Dashboard, Help & Money Map, Income & Paycheck, Expenses & Rent, Profile & Assumptions and Settings are always shown; the Retirement Tax Plan only appears for US profiles). Right-click a screen in the sidebar and choose *Hide from sidebar* for a quick change.
- **Cards inside screens:** turn off Dashboard, Budget, Investments and Taxes cards you don't use. Dashboard figures about retirement and taxes also follow your layout.

Nothing is deleted; hidden screens leave the sidebar and the Go menu but links to them still work. Each profile has its own layout, saved with the profile so it syncs.

## Screens

### Dashboard
KPI tiles, *Where your paycheck goes* (donut from gross pay: taxes, housing, living costs, EMIs, retirement, benefits, saving, unassigned), top spending categories, a **spending trend** you can shape (pick 1M, 3M, 6M, YTD, 1Y, 2Y or your own dates; group by day, week, two weeks or month; stack by category or show totals; tap **Flexible** to hide fixed bills like rent, or a category to see only that; tap a bar for its breakdown and how it compares with your average), the **financial health score** (0–100, with points per area and your biggest opportunity), net worth history (recorded monthly), a retirement fan chart and the top savings opportunities with $/month impact.
 **Milestones** shows the next milestones within reach (net worth, emergency fund, debt-free, saving 20%, invested, credit score) and the ones you've reached with the date; you're notified when you reach a new one.
### Help & Money Map
How everything in your finances fits together, as an interactive map: **Income** splits into **Taxes**, **Paycheck savings** (401(k), HSA, insurance) and **Take-home pay**; take-home pays for **Needs**, **Wants**, **Debt payments**, **Saving** and **Investing**; those build **Cash & savings**, **Investments**, **Home & other assets** and shrink **What you owe**; together they make your **Net worth** and **Your future**. **Employer stock** vests into what you own, and **Protection** (insurance and an emergency plan) guards it. Each circle shows your own number. Click a circle to see what it is, tips, the words to know and where the money comes from and goes — its lines light up, are labelled and show money moving along them; click a link to follow it, or **Open** to go to its screen. **Walk me through it** steps through all 17 parts in order. Below the map: five steps to get started, the **Sample Profile**, help for when you don't know a number, and **Money words, explained** — a searchable glossary of 60+ terms (APR, 401(k), vesting, net worth…) in plain English.

### Starting Out
Money basics for a first job, a first paycheck — or no job yet. **First steps** tracks your progress through seven of them (a bank account, writing down income, tracking spending, an emergency fund, a goal, credit, investing early), each opening the screen where you do it. **Your paycheck, explained** shows pay before tax, what's taken out and what reaches your bank — and tells you when you earn under the tax-free amount, so tax taken from your pay should come back. **Building credit** explains how to start and what helps your score. Calculators show **what a car really costs** (loan, insurance, fuel, upkeep and lost value, as a share of take-home pay), **paying for college** (after grants, saved monthly or borrowed) and **why starting early matters** (the same monthly amount from now versus ten years later). In the Student and Simple layouts.

### Money Boxes
Your money as boxes inside boxes — the bigger the box, the more money. **Money in & out**: your income is the big box, with **Taxes**, **Paycheck savings & benefits** and **Take-home pay** inside; take-home holds **Needs**, **Wants**, **Debt payments**, **Saving**, **Investing**, **Property costs** and what's **Not assigned yet**, and those open into each category, tax and debt. Boxes with boxes inside show them faintly; click one to open it, and use the path at the top to go back out. Monthly or yearly. If you plan to spend more than you take home, a red note says by how much. **What you own & owe** shows your balances the same way: cash by kind of account, investments by account, vested company stock, home and property, valuables — next to every debt. Below the boxes, a list with bars, and how take-home compares with the 50/30/20 guide.

### My Boards
Your own screens. With no board yet, pick a template (*Money in & out*, *Student budget*, *Saving & net worth*) or *Blank*. **+** adds a widget, with a live preview while you set it up:
- **Number** — money in or out, recurring bills, everyday spending, spent this month, saved, savings rate, left over, net worth, cash, investments, debt, loan payments, emergency-fund months, one spending category (yours included), a savings goal, or a number you type (money, a plain number or a percentage).
- **Graph** — choose the data (by category, by source, where the money goes, money in vs out by month, spending or income by month, net worth over time, investments by asset class, or your own labels and values), the style (bars, line, area, donut) and, for graphs over time, how many months. For spending by month, tick categories to compare them.
- **Note** — any text you want to keep in view.

Give a widget a title (or leave it blank for an automatic one), a color and a size (small widgets share a row; full-width ones get their own). Tap a widget to change it; right-click or long-press to move it earlier or later, resize, duplicate or remove it. The **Board** menu renames or deletes the board and makes new ones; switch between boards at the top.

### Reports & Digest
**Weekly digest:** this week's spending against a typical week (the 12 weeks before), income received, how net worth moved since the last monthly record, what's due in the next 7 days, budget warnings, the next life event and goal progress. Turn on *Send me the digest every week* and pick the day and time — the notification is made on the device with your latest numbers each time the app opens. **PDF report:** *Save PDF…* (or *Prepare to share*) makes a four-page report — summary with this week, cash flow, balance sheet with holdings, debts and real estate, and the plan with success odds, life events and goals. It contains your financial details; share it only with people you trust.

### Spending Insights
**Where your money goes** is a flow diagram of a typical month: where money comes from on the left (salary, self-employment, rent, other income — and a *Shortfall* if you spend more than comes in), your income in the middle, and where it goes on the right (taxes, retirement from pay, health and benefits, your largest spending categories with the small ones grouped, property costs, loan and card payments, savings & investing, and what's left over). **This year vs last year** compares spending by month and totals the last 12 complete months against the 12 before; **Biggest changes** lists the categories that moved most. **Spending by tag** totals what each tag cost over the same period. **Top merchants** groups one-off spending by store (and bills by their name) for 3, 6 or 12 months, with the number of payments, the average, and an arrow when you've spent noticeably more or less there in the last 3 months.

### Income & Paycheck
Add any income: salary (any pay frequency), part-time or gig work and bonuses (taxed like wages), self-employment / freelance, stock sales / RSUs / options, rent, dividends, pensions, Social Security, unemployment / disability / benefits, scholarships / grants / stipends, allowance / family support, child support / alimony, gifts / inheritances or anything else (each type notes how it's usually taxed; switch *Taxable* if yours is different) — or pick one of your own categories (*New category…* makes one on the spot). Choose **Regularly** with a start date and, for a contract or job that stops, an end date — or **One time** with the date it arrives. One-time money isn't part of your monthly pay: money still to come is added to Projections and Life Events on its date (after an estimate of tax), and money already received shows in *One-time, last 12 mo*. Recurring income that hasn't started yet, or has ended, doesn't count toward today's pay; projections pick up the change on its date. The *Income mix* donut shows where monthly income comes from, including rent from your properties. Mark who it belongs to once you've set up your household. For salary enter your 401(k)/pension % (pre-tax or Roth), employer match (e.g. 100% up to 6%), and per-paycheck deductions (health premium, HSA, FSA/commuter, post-tax). The paycheck breakdown chart shows exactly where each paycheck goes.

### Taxes
Annual estimate with federal/national, state/regional, Social Security/social insurance, Medicare and self-employment tax; effective and marginal rates; a bracket chart; and a *what-if* slider showing how much tax extra pre-tax contributions save. With a W-2 on file (US) it compares withholding vs. estimated liability.
 **Quarterly estimated tax** (US) works out the tax on income without withholding (self-employment, rent, dividends, benefits) and splits it across the four due dates; *Mark paid* as you pay. Payments are needed when you'd owe $1,000 or more.
**Tax rules:** the *Tax rules* card shows the bands, allowance and contributions in use. *Edit tax rules…* lets you change any of them — every box explains what it means with an example — and *Use WealthPilot's values again* undoes it. Living somewhere WealthPilot doesn't cover? Choose **Other country** in Profile and enter your country's name, currency and rules here. *How this is worked out* lists the official sources and what the estimate leaves out.

### Pay Statements (US: W-2)
Optional — in the US, W-2s let WealthPilot check your withholding. Click **Enter manually** (or **+**) and copy each number from your W-2 into the box with the same number. W-2s are **typed in, not scanned**: photos and PDFs were sometimes misread, and one wrong digit changes your tax picture. Every box has **ⓘ** explaining what it is and where it sits on the form, and *Where is everything on a W-2?* shows a sketch of the form.

Only **Box 1** and the tax year are required — and Box 1 can be **Don't know** for now. Any box can be *Don't know*; for Box 4 and Box 6 the app uses 6.2% and 1.45% of the wages until you type the real number. The W-2 list marks unknown boxes, and the Inputs Checklist lists them. The boxes are checked against each other (Box 4 ≈ 6.2% of Box 3, Box 6 ≈ 1.45% of Box 5, Box 3 under the year's Social Security limit, Box 1 ≤ Box 5, state boxes without a state, Box 17 > Box 16, the same employer and year twice) — these are warnings, so you can still save a W-2 that really is unusual. Worked in two states? Add the second state line as a second W-2. You can also create a salary income from the W-2 (from Box 5, or Box 1 if Box 5 is empty).

### Pay Statements (outside the US)
The same **Pay Statements** screen shows your country's year-end form instead of a W-2: a **P60** (UK), **Employment Detail Summary** from myAccount (Ireland), **IRP5** (South Africa), **summary of earnings** from myIR (New Zealand), **Form 16** (India), **income statement** (Australia), **T4** (Canada), **Lohnsteuerbescheinigung** (Germany), **IR8A** (Singapore), **gensen chōshū-hyō** (Japan) or a **salary certificate** (UAE). Every line is explained (tap ⓘ), any line can be *Don't know*, and the card compares the tax taken with WealthPilot's estimate for that country.

### Expenses & Rent
Recurring bills (with frequency, start/end dates) and one-off purchases. Mark items essential vs. discretionary. A recurring bill's *Remind me* sets its own reminder (or follows the default in Settings). The Rent card shows housing as % of gross pay, the 30% affordability limit, your state's rough median rent and 5-year rent cost. The 50/30/20 card compares needs/wants/savings with targets.

**Tags, splits and shared costs:** in an expense, add *Tags* (comma-separated) — the chips above the list filter by tag, and search matches names, tags and notes. For a one-off expense, *Split across categories…* divides it into parts that add up to the purchase (each part becomes its own expense, marked with a split icon; *Join the parts back together* undoes it). Turn on *Someone owes me part of this* to enter the total paid, what's owed and who owes it — your share is what counts as spending; *Owed to you* above the list totals it by person with *Paid back*, and *They won't pay* moves it into your spending. *Attach a receipt* keeps a photo or PDF with the expense on this device (a paperclip shows in the list).
**Import a statement:** *+ → Import bank or card statement…* takes a CSV, OFX, QFX or QIF file from your bank's or card's website, or a transactions export from Mint, YNAB, Monarch or Copilot (their categories, tags and notes come across). Review every line before importing: ticked lines become one-off expenses with a guessed category (tap the category to change it — other lines from the same merchant follow, and the app remembers it). Lines already recorded, recurring bills you already track, card payments and transfers start unticked; money in is listed separately and isn't imported. If the file doesn't say which way round amounts are, flip *Purchases are positive amounts in this file*. Learned merchants are listed under *Categories → Import rules*.
**Receipts:** type the expense in, then *Attach receipt* to keep a photo or PDF of it with the expense. WealthPilot doesn't read receipts — a blurry photo could give a wrong amount, so you enter the numbers yourself.

### Monthly Plan
Everything you pay regularly, in one place: money in (take-home), recurring costs, everyday spending, what's saved and what's left over each month; a chart of where each month's money goes; recurring costs by category (yours included) with each category's share of your take-home and the bills in it; and what you save each month, with a link to change it. **Edit monthly costs** opens one form for all recurring costs — change a name, category, amount, how often it's paid or whether it's essential; **Add a cost** in any category (or *New category…*); and *I no longer pay this* ends a cost today so past months keep it. Common costs (rent, utilities, insurance, phone, transport, groceries, subscriptions) are offered as empty rows — leave them at 0 to skip.

### Budget & Bills
**Budgets:** use the toolbar's *Edit budgets* to set a monthly limit per category (leave 0 to skip one), or *Suggest from the last 3 months* to start from your averages for flexible spending. Each bar shows spent vs. limit; the tick marks how far through the month you are, so a bar past the tick is ahead of pace. Recurring bills count in full for the month; one-off purchases count as logged. Orange means you're on pace to exceed the budget, red means you already have.
**Safe to spend:** take-home pay this month, less bills (in full), loan and card payments, and savings and investing contributions, is your flexible money for the month; one-off spending so far comes off it. The daily figure shares what was left this morning evenly across the days left, so overspending one day lowers the next. **Every dollar has a job:** this month's take-home split across bills, debt payments, saving, property costs and your budgets (each budget counts only what isn't already covered by bills in that category). Anything unassigned gets buttons to budget it, save it or pay down debt; if you've planned more than you take home, trim until it balances.
**Rollover budgets:** in *Edit budgets*, turn on *Roll over to next month* under a category. Each month's unspent amount is added to the next month's limit and overspending is taken off; the bar shows "+$X rolled over" or "−$X overspent before". Today's limit is used for earlier months, and at most 12 months carry. **Alerts:** the line under the budgets shows whether budget alerts are on — *Turn on…* opens Settings.
**Bills calendar:** a month grid of bills, EMIs and paychecks — orange is money out, green money in. Use ‹ › to change month and tap a day to list what's on it.
**Upcoming & checking forecast:** paychecks (after tax and deductions), recurring bills and EMIs by date for the next 2 weeks, 30 days or 60 days. The chart starts from your checking accounts' balance and steps through each day; the lowest point is shown, with a warning if it goes below zero. Transfers to savings and investments aren't included because their dates aren't known.

### Subscriptions
Streaming, apps, memberships and anything else that renews by itself. Recurring expenses in the Subscriptions category, or from well-known services (Netflix, Spotify, iCloud, Prime, Adobe…) in any category, are listed with their monthly and yearly cost and next renewal; a bell means a reminder is set. Tap one to edit it; right-click it to *Mark as cancelled* (no more renewals from today — past months keep their cost). **Found in your spending** lists merchants charged at a steady rhythm and amount among your one-off expenses (usually from imported statements) that aren't tracked yet, with any price rise; *Track as subscription* adds a recurring expense from the next charge (later statement imports then skip those charges), *Not a subscription* hides the suggestion. Add one by hand with **+**.

### Categories
Your own categories for spending and income — make as many as you like (*New category*), each with a name, color and symbol. A spending category **counts as** a built-in one (and can be marked essential), so budgets, the 50/30/20 check and needs vs wants keep working; an income category is **taxed like** the income type you pick. Each row shows how many records use it and its monthly total. Tap one to rename or change it (its records move along); the trash button deletes it — its income and spending stay, under the built-in category it counted as.

**Your form fields:** add your own fields to income, expenses, debts, cash accounts, investments, goals, property or W-2s — text, a number, money, a date, yes / no, or a list of choices — each with an optional hint and marked required or optional. Add one from any form (*Add your own field…* near the bottom) or here; tap one to rename it, change its hint or choices, or remove it (values you typed are kept but no longer shown). Filled-in fields show on income and expense rows and Expenses search finds them.

### Debts & EMIs
Each loan's EMI is computed from balance, APR and remaining months (or enter your actual payment). Tap a debt to see its amortization (principal vs. interest by year with the remaining balance). *Payoff strategy* compares minimum payments, Avalanche (highest rate first) and Snowball (smallest balance first) with an adjustable extra payment.
 **Credit card utilization:** enter a card's *Credit limit* in its editor (pencil button) to see its balance as a share of the limit and overall utilization — under 30% helps your credit score, under 10% is best.

**Student loan repayment plans** appear when you have a student loan: every plan side by side with its monthly payment (first and last), how long it runs, the total you'd pay and anything forgiven at the end. In the US: Standard, Graduated, Extended (over $30,000), Income-Based (IBR) and the Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP), under 2026 rules, with a note on Public Service Loan Forgiveness. In the UK: Plan 2 and Plan 5 (9% of income above the threshold, written off after 30 or 40 years). Elsewhere: a standard and a longer term. Move the income slider to see how a raise or a pay cut changes things. Estimates only — check studentaid.gov or the Student Loans Company before switching.
### Savings & Goals
Accounts with APY and monthly contributions; flag your emergency fund. CDs, fixed deposits, T-bills and **bonds held directly** can have a **maturity date** — the list shows when each matures and the Dashboard reminds you 90 days ahead. *Property & other assets* takes a home, rental, land, vehicle or business, and also **gold & precious metals**, **collectibles, art & jewelry**, **private / startup investments**, **life insurance cash value** and **annuities** — each with a hint on how to value it and a typical yearly change to start from. A 5-year chart compares your current rates with the best typical high-yield rate. Goals show progress and the monthly saving needed to hit the target date.

### Investments
Holdings by account type (401(k), IRA, Roth, brokerage, HSA, 529, pension, crypto, real estate) and asset class. Optionally give a holding a **ticker, share count and price** — its value becomes shares × price; *Get price* in the editor, or *Prices & rates → Refresh prices* for all of them, fetches the latest price (only the ticker is sent, to Yahoo Finance; add an exchange suffix such as .L or .NS outside the US). Holdings, savings accounts and other assets can be in **another currency**: pick it in the editor and enter the amount in that currency; it's converted to your home currency everywhere at the rates in *Prices & rates → Exchange rates…* (type your own, or *Get latest rates* — European Central Bank reference rates). A *By currency* card shows how much you hold in each. Shows current vs. recommended allocation (age- and risk-based glide path), rebalancing suggestions, a risk/return scatter vs. model portfolios, tax-loss harvesting candidates (taxable holdings below cost, with the estimated tax saved), and the prioritized contribution ladder (emergency fund → match → high-interest debt → HSA → IRA → 401(k) max → taxable).

**Your other wealth** sums what's outside investment accounts — vested company stock, property and valuables — with a link to each. **One company, how much of your money?** flags any single company (stocks you hold by ticker plus vested company stock) above 10% of your cash and investments, and above 20% as high; broad index funds such as VTI or VOO aren't counted as one company.

### Stock Grants & Options
For stock from your employer. Add each grant from your stock plan website (Fidelity, Schwab, E*TRADE, Morgan Stanley, Carta): **RSUs**, **stock options (NSO or ISO)** with their strike price, **ESPP** shares, or **shares you own** in a private company. Enter the shares, today's price, shares already sold and the vesting — start date, total length, cliff, and how often it vests after the cliff (monthly, quarterly, every 6 months or yearly). The screen shows what's **yours now** (vested, at today's price — options only count the amount above the strike), what's **not yet vested** (yours only if you stay), and **what vests in the next year** with a calendar. RSUs are taxed as pay when they vest: the app estimates that tax and warns when your employer's usual 22% federal withholding won't cover it. Vested shares count toward net worth unless you switch that off (when you've also added them as a holding). Each kind of grant is explained at the bottom.

### Net Worth History
Your net worth month by month — what you owned, what you owed and the difference — as a chart and a list. The app records it each month you open it; **Add a past month** fills in earlier months (from old statements or a past tax return) so the trend starts years back. {Tap} a month to change or delete it.

### Performance & Dividends
Uses holdings with a ticker and share count. Tap **Update market data** (top right) to fetch five years of daily prices and dividends for your tickers and the benchmark — only the ticker symbols are sent, to Yahoo Finance, and the data is kept on this device for 12 hours. **Performance vs benchmark** shows the growth of 100 for what you hold today (same share counts throughout, dividends reinvested) against the S&P 500, total world stocks, the Nasdaq-100 or a 60/40 fund, over 1M–5Y; buys, sells and new money aren't modelled. **Dividends:** the next 12 months by month and a calendar of expected ex-dividend dates (payments usually arrive a few weeks later), projected from each holding's usual rhythm and latest payout; holdings that stopped paying aren't projected. **Allocation drift** compares your mix with the recommended one; turn on *Alert me when it drifts* and pick how many points. **Price alerts:** in a holding's editor set *Alert when above* and/or *below*; WealthPilot checks those prices when you open it (at most every 4 hours) and when you refresh prices, and notifies you once each time a level is crossed.

### Real Estate
Where you live and what you own. **Where you live** is worked out from your records: rent you pay (an Expenses item under Rent) means *renting*; a home you add as *Home I live in* means owning — *with a home loan* if a mortgage is linked to it (or you have a mortgage that isn't linked to another property), otherwise *outright*. You can rent where you live and own rentals, or live in your own home and rent out another.
Add a property with *Add property*: the home you live in, a **rental property** (house or apartment you rent out), a second home or land. Link its **loan** (or add one), set its value and yearly growth, and — for a rental — the rent, how much of the year it's usually empty, property tax, insurance, HOA, upkeep (% of value) and a manager's fee. You'll see rent after empty months, running costs, the loan payment and the **cash flow** left, **cap rate** (rent minus costs ÷ value), **cash-on-cash** (yearly cash flow ÷ your equity), gross yield and **total return** (adding value growth and the principal you pay down), plus equity over the next 10 years. Rent counts as income in your pay and taxes (profit after costs and loan interest is taxed; depreciation isn't modelled), rental costs come out of your cash flow, and a rental's loan payment is not counted as your own housing cost. Bills for the home you live in belong in Expenses → Housing.

### Projections
Year-by-year projection plus a 1,000-scenario Monte Carlo fan chart in today's money. What-if sliders: extra monthly investing, retirement age, return, retirement spending, inflation. The **life events timeline** card shows your planned events and future income changes, the balance at retirement and success odds with and without them, and a switch to leave them out of the projections; event markers also appear on the expected path. Milestone table every 5 years.

### Life Events
Plan the big moments and see what they do to your future. *Add life event* (or a chip under the timeline) starts from a typical amount for a home, rental, sale, baby, wedding, college, car, new job, career break, business, inheritance, move, caring for a parent, medical cost or big trip — change the date, **money once** (costs or brings in) and a **monthly change** (costs more, or saves/earns more) for a set time, until you retire, or for good. Amounts are in today's money.
- **Timeline:** one row per event by age — a dot for one-time money (bigger for larger amounts) and a bar for changes that last. Tap one to edit it.
- **With vs without events:** the expected path both ways, with markers where events happen; plus **cash flow from events** per year, the heaviest year and the lowest balance before you retire, **plan with vs without** (balance at retirement, success odds, sustainable spending, financial-independence age) and the **cost by kind**.
- **Event by event:** each event's own effect on your balance at retirement, and a warning if the cash you'll have by then (savings plus what's left over each month) won't cover its one-time cost.
- Ongoing costs come first out of what's left over each month (cash flow you're not already saving) and only the rest out of savings; one-time amounts move savings directly. A down payment leaves your investments (the home's value isn't counted as spendable).
- Future one-time income and jobs that start or end (from Income) appear automatically, marked *from Income* — edit them there.

### Retirement
Readiness (projected vs. needed), budget split between benefits and portfolio, income-by-source chart, and a comparison of withdrawal strategies with success rates and spending ranges. The bucket strategy splits the nest egg into cash (years 1–2), bonds (3–10) and growth (11+).

### Retirement Tax Plan
US rules, in today's money with 2026 brackets (assumed to rise with inflation), federal plus your state. **When to claim Social Security:** uses the benefit in Profile & Assumptions to show what you'd get each month at every claiming age from 62 to 70 (about 30% less at 62, 24% more at 70 than at 67), the total collected by your life expectancy, and the break-even age; pick the age to plan with. **Which accounts to draw first** splits your accounts into taxable (brokerage, cash), tax-deferred (401(k), traditional IRA, pension) and tax-free (Roth, HSA) and runs your retirement year by year three ways: taxable first, a bit from each, or tax-deferred up to the top of the 12% bracket first. Compare lifetime tax, how long the money lasts and what's left after tax; tap one to chart its balances. **Required minimum distributions** start at 73 (born 1951–59) or 75 (born 1960 or later) and are shown for the first years. **Roth conversions** move tax-deferred money to Roth each year until RMDs start, up to the top of the bracket you choose, and compare the result with not converting. Spousal benefits, Medicare surcharges and the 5-year rule aren't modelled — check big decisions with a tax professional.

### Money Map
A sandbox for trying out where your money could go — nothing here changes your real investments. On the left are your **take-home pay** (and how much is free to invest each month after spending, debt payments and savings), your **wealth** (savings and investments today) and your **spending**. Add **boxes** with *Add box* — index fund, dividend stocks, bond fund, CDs/Treasuries, real estate (REIT), crypto, high-yield savings or custom. Each starts with typical return, income (yield), ups and downs and fees for your country; tap a box to change them, rename it, or mark it tax-sheltered.
- **Link money in:** drag from the ● handle on *Take-home* onto a box to invest every month, or from *Your wealth* to move money in once. Tap a link's label to change the amount or remove it. The *Unlinked each month* tile and a red warning show if links add up to more than you have.
- **Boxes feeding boxes:** drag from a box's handle onto another box to send it that box's **income** (e.g. dividends into an index fund), or switch the link to **move everything** in a chosen year (e.g. a CD ladder maturing into stocks). Loops aren't allowed.
- **Watch it grow:** drag the year slider or press ▶. Boxes show their median value, the income they pay and a 10th–90th percentile range; the outcome box adds everything up. Values are in today's money across 500 simulated markets in which stocks, bonds and the other boxes move together realistically; income from boxes that aren't tax-sheltered is taxed each year.
- **Compare:** the KPIs and charts compare your boxes with keeping the same money in a savings account and with one target-date fund (65/35) on the same markets.
- **Get started fast:** *Start with an example* builds three linked boxes sized to your numbers; *Import my holdings* adds a box per holding, linked with its balance and monthly contribution.
- The map is saved with the profile (and so synced and backed up). Use *Scenarios* to keep several maps, e.g. "Aggressive" and "Safe". On iPhone the map is shown as a top-to-bottom list; tap a box to link money in.

### Emergency Plan
If your income stopped tomorrow: **how long you could last** — spending as you do now, cut to essentials, adding taxable investments (at a cautious 75% of today's value), and adding retirement accounts after tax and penalty — with a verdict from *strong* (six months or more of essentials) to *very little cushion*. Enter any **income you'd still have** (a partner's pay, unemployment benefits) to see the difference. **What to cut first** lists pausing saving and investing, then your wants, largest first. **The order to follow** walks through the steps: pause saving, cut wants, apply for help, call lenders, live on the emergency fund, then taxable investments, retirement money last.

### Insurance Check
Add the insurance you have — term or whole life, disability, health, homeowners, renters, auto, umbrella, long-term care — with coverage, monthly premium and whether it's through work. The check compares it with what your situation usually calls for: life cover of about 10 years of pay plus debts, less savings, when someone depends on you; disability cover of about 60% of pay; health; home or renters cover; auto liability of at least $100,000; umbrella cover once your net worth passes $500,000; long-term care from your 50s. Each is marked **Covered**, **Gap**, **Missing**, **Optional** or **Not needed now**, with the reason and an **Add** button. Rules of thumb only — an agent or advisor can size cover properly.

### Estate & Records
**Documents:** the essentials (will, financial power of attorney, healthcare proxy, living will, beneficiary designations, letter of instruction) are listed for you; add others (trust, life insurance, digital accounts list, guardian for children) with **+**. Tap one to set its status, where the original is and when you last reviewed it — done documents are flagged for review after 3 years. *Attach a copy* keeps a PDF or photo on this device only (not synced or backed up by WealthPilot); right-click a document to view its copy. Never store passwords here. **Beneficiaries:** name who each retirement and bank account goes to (as set with the bank or plan — that designation overrides your will) and your executor. **Credit score:** *Add a score* to log readings; the chart shows the trend against the Poor–Exceptional bands, with tips for raising it.

### Document Vault
Keep statements, tax forms and returns, insurance policies, wills, loan papers, pay stubs and copies of IDs with your plan. Add a PDF or other file, or a photo; the kind and year are guessed from the file name and you can change them. Documents are grouped by kind; **View** opens one, and you can share it. With iCloud sync on, **documents and their files sync to all your devices** through your private iCloud; a document added elsewhere shows *in iCloud — tap View to download* until you open it. Each device keeps its copy locked while the device is locked. Files can be up to 50 MB. Deleting a document removes it from every device. With sync off, documents stay on this device. Backups, exports, household files and profile copies include the files, so restoring a backup brings your documents back. Copies attached to estate documents appear here too. The app never reads numbers from documents — type them into the forms yourself.

### Investment Strategies
**Your strategies:** most people follow more than one approach — say a three-fund portfolio in the 401(k) and IRA, and dividend growth in a brokerage account. Tap *Add a strategy*, give it a name, pick the strategy and tick the holdings that follow it. Each holding follows one strategy, so ticking one that's already mapped moves it (the editor shows *moves from …*). The bar at the top shows how your portfolio splits between strategies, and anything not yet mapped is listed underneath. Tap a strategy to model it with **its holdings' own numbers**: their balance, their monthly contributions (including their share of payroll 401(k) contributions and match), their fund fees and current mix — and no dividend tax when every holding is in a tax-sheltered account. Use the pencil to rename it, change holdings or delete it (your holdings stay). Holdings show their strategy as a tag under *Investments → Holdings*, and strategies are saved with the profile, so they sync, back up and copy with it.

**All strategies together (mix and match):** once you have a strategy, tap *All strategies together* at the top of the list. Every strategy — plus holdings not mapped to one — is modeled as one portfolio until retirement, on the same simulated markets as a single target-date fund. Set how much you invest each month and slide each strategy's **share of new money** (shares are scaled to 100%; today's balances stay put). The building-block mix (US/international stocks, bonds, gold, cash, individual picks & crypto, bond ladder), expected return and volatility, the median and bad-case value at retirement, the bad-case drop in the last 5 years, the chance of beating a target-date fund, and the chart all update as you move the sliders. Your slider settings are saved with the profile, so they're there next time and sync, back up and copy with it; strategies added later start from what they get today. *Reset everything* puts the sliders back and forgets the saved mix.

**Moving today's money:** turn on *Move today's balances between strategies* to give each strategy a target share of what you hold today. Each one shows what it would buy or sell; the total moved and an **estimated tax** on the sales appear below (sales come from 401(k), IRA and HSA money first, where there's no tax, then taxable holdings in proportion, with their gains taxed at the long-term rate — 15% in the US). The projection then compares *Your combined plan* (after the move and the tax) with *As held today* and one target-date fund, and shows how often the move ends ahead. *Match the new-money shares* copies the new-money split; *Back to today's balances* undoes the move. In practice money doesn't move between account types — you change what each account holds — so do the selling inside retirement accounts where you can.

**Strategies available:** the options **wheel**, **dollar-cost averaging**, **dividend growth**, the **three-fund index portfolio**, and:
- **Target-date glide path** — start mostly in stocks and step toward bonds every year until you retire. Compared on the same simulated markets with staying at today's mix and with staying at the retirement mix, including the bad-case drop in the last 5 years before retirement.
- **Core & satellite** — a broad index fund plus a slice of stocks or themes you pick. Set the satellite's share, how much better (or worse) your picks do than the market, how much they swing and what they cost; see how often the mix ends ahead of all-index.
- **Bond / CD ladder** — bonds or CDs maturing one per year. Spend each rung as it matures (income ladder, compared with a level annuity-style payout) or reinvest it at the top (rolling ladder); see the cash each year, the average yield and what the last year is worth after inflation.
- **All-Weather & Permanent portfolio** — fixed shares of stocks, long-term bonds, gold/commodities and cash, rebalanced yearly, compared with 100% stocks and 60/40 for growth, bad case, worst drop and the chance of ending below what you put in.

**Explore:** tap any strategy to model it with your whole portfolio, then *Add as my strategy* to map holdings to it. Every slider stays yours to change.

- **Wheel (options income).** Model the options “wheel”: sell a cash-secured put; if assigned, sell covered calls above your cost basis; if called away, start again. Adjust price, implied volatility, strike distances, days to expiry, drift, rate and horizon. See per-trade quotes (premium, delta, assignment probability, annualized return), a Monte Carlo comparison against buy-and-hold, drawdowns, and an estimate of monthly income from an options sleeve of your portfolio. Read the risk notes — the wheel keeps most of the stock's downside while capping its upside.
- **Dollar-cost averaging.** Should a lump sum (a bonus, inheritance, idle cash) go in today or in equal monthly slices? Set the amount (prefilled from cash earning little), how many months to spread it over, the horizon, expected return, volatility and what the waiting cash earns. 800 simulated markets show the median and bad-case (10th percentile) results for both, and how often investing at once ends ahead.
- **Dividend growth.** Start amount (your taxable brokerage), monthly investment, starting yield, dividend growth, share-price growth, tax on dividends and reinvesting (DRIP). See after-tax income by year, portfolio value, yield on cost, and the year dividends would cover your spending — today's spending grown with inflation.
- **Three-fund index portfolio.** A US total-market fund, an international fund and a bond fund at weights you choose (prefilled from your age/risk glide path). Compares never rebalancing, rebalancing yearly and rebalancing when any fund drifts 5 points, on the same simulated markets: median and bad-case value, worst drop and where your stock share ends up. *What fees cost you* compares index-fund fees with your current funds over the same years.

### Rent vs Buy
Compares the net worth of buying (equity after selling costs + invested savings) against renting and investing the difference, with a breakeven year.

### Calculators
Loan/EMI with prepayment, SIP/compound growth with annual step-up, inflation, FIRE (years to financial independence), and life insurance needs (income to replace, mortgage, other debts, education and final expenses, minus savings and existing cover — prefilled from your plan).

### AI Advisor
Ask natural-language questions such as *“Where am I overspending?”* or *“Should I pay off debt or invest?”*, or pick one of 120 starter questions in 11 categories — Popular, Spending & budget, Saving, Debt, Investing, Retirement, Taxes, Income & career, Family & kids, Wealth & net worth and Big decisions (e.g. *“What if I retired 5 years earlier?”*, *“Should I pay off my mortgage early or invest?”*, *“How much will a child cost me each year?”*, *“What happens to my money if the stock market drops 30%?”*, *“Is a job offer paying 15% more worth it?”*, *“Should I itemize or take the standard deduction?”*, *“When could I become a millionaire?”*). Every question comes with figures the app computes from your data. Answers always use your latest numbers: the conversation stays while you go and change things elsewhere in the app (add an investment, raise the rent…), and when you come back a bar shows what changed since the last answer — e.g. *Rent $2,850/mo → $3,100/mo* — with **Update answer** to ask the same question again. The new answer starts with a note listing what changed. The question list is the main screen: asking a question opens the conversation, **Back** returns to the questions (your category stays selected) and **Continue** reopens the conversation; under each answer are related questions to ask next. *New conversation* starts over.
For each question the app first computes the exact figures itself (debt payoff times, the extra saving needed to retire on time, tax saved, an affordable house price…) and gives them to Apple's on-device language model together with a compact summary of your numbers (viewable under *What the advisor sees*), so answers quote real figures instead of the model's own arithmetic. Typed questions are matched to the closest topic by keywords. Nothing is sent to a server. If Apple Intelligence is unavailable, you get the same figures as a rule-based answer to your question.

### Household
Add the people you share money with (*Add member*: name, color, symbol). In any income, expense, account, holding, asset or debt editor, **Belongs to** marks it as one person's or *Shared*. The screen shows each person's income, one-time income, spending, cash, investments, property and debts and net worth side by side (*Yours, mine & ours*), and **who pays the shared bills** — split in proportion to income or evenly. Removing a member makes what they owned shared.
**Sharing with a partner:** your own devices sync through iCloud automatically. For a partner on their own Apple Account, *Send household file…* saves the whole profile as a file to send by AirDrop, Messages, Mail or Files. On their device, *Open a household file…* adds it as a profile; later files update that same profile, matching records one by one so nothing is duplicated (*Add new and changed records*), or make it an exact copy of the file. Either of you can send updates back. Nothing goes through a server.

### Profile & Assumptions
Country, state, filing status, dependents, ages, risk tolerance (with a 5-question quiz), retirement spending, Social Security/pension estimate, salary growth, and optional overrides for inflation and expected return. Data tools (backup, restore, sample data, erase) live in **Settings**.

### Settings
Open from the sidebar or with **⌘,**.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Sync with iCloud** | Stores your plan in your private iCloud (CloudKit) database and syncs it across devices signed in to the same Apple Account. Shows account state, sync activity and last sync time. Turning it off keeps a local copy. |
| **Require Face ID / Touch ID** | Locks the app on launch and when it returns from the background (falls back to device passcode). |

| **Hide amounts** | Privacy mode — masks every money value on screen. |
| **Theme / Open app to** | Light, dark or system appearance; the screen shown at launch. |
| **Reminders** | Monthly money check-in (choose the day) and weekly spending log (choose the weekday), at a time you pick. **Bill reminders** before each recurring bill and EMI (on the day or 1–7 days before; a bill can override this in its editor under *Remind me*). **Budget alerts** when a category reaches your warning level and when it goes over. |
| **On-device AI analysis** | Turn Apple Intelligence features off to use rule-based analysis only. |
| **Monte Carlo scenarios** | 500 – 5,000 simulated markets for projections. |
| **Data** | Export a JSON backup, restore from a backup, export expenses as CSV, load sample data, or erase everything. |
| **Diagnostics** | Detailed logging, export diagnostic logs (errors, crashes, hangs, sync events — no amounts), show logs in Finder, clear logs. |

Device-specific preferences (theme, lock, reminders) stay on each device; your financial data is what syncs.
